## How the video is made

Every Hotel Lobby AI video is built in two stages:

1. **The scene.** An image model places person A on the left and person B on the right inside the orange booth, with the microphone hanging between them. This sets faces, outfits and framing.
2. **The motion.** A motion-transfer model carries the reference performance — head moves, gestures, lip-sync and audio — onto that scene.

Because the scene comes first, most problems in the final video start in the photos. Fix the inputs and the output follows.

## Step 1: Choose the right two photos

The photos set the ceiling for the whole video. A good pair has:

- **A full, sharp face** — no sunglasses, masks, heavy filters or hair across the eyes.
- **A similar crop.** Two head-and-shoulders shots, or two half-body shots. A close-up next to a full-body photo is the most common cause of one person looking oversized.
- **Similar lighting and angle.** Two front-facing daylight photos blend better than one selfie and one dark party photo.
- **A plain background** and only one person in each photo.
- **A neutral or soft expression**, which leaves room for the model to animate believable reactions.

Accepted formats are JPG, PNG and WebP up to 10 MB each. Only use photos you have permission to use.

## Step 2: Set up the duet

On the [generator](/#create):

- **Person A (left)** usually leads the first line; **person B (right)** reacts and takes the next one. Swap the photos to swap the roles.
- **Framing:** "Classic" (knees up) keeps faces larger and is the safer first try. "Full body" shows outfits and shoes but makes faces smaller.
- **Length:** 8 seconds matches the core of the performance; 15 seconds gives a longer clip and costs more credits.

## Step 3: Write a short prompt (or skip it)

The base prompt already fixes the booth, the centered mic, the vertical frame and left-right placement. The styling note is only for one extra detail. Good examples:

- "Both wearing black sunglasses."
- "Matching white T-shirts."
- "Person A in a red jacket."

If you use a general AI video tool instead, you need the full shot list. This is the starter prompt we use:

> Two people from my two reference photos perform a duet in a seamless burnt-orange recording booth. Person A (photo 1) stands on the left, Person B (photo 2) on the right, facing each other across one black microphone hanging at face level in the exact center. Vertical 9:16, static centered camera, soft even studio light, framed from the knees up. Keep each face, hairstyle and outfit unchanged. No side swaps, no extra people, no captions, no watermark.

## Step 4: Generate and review

Press Generate. The scene appears in under a minute, and the animated video follows in about 3–5 minutes. You can close the tab; finished videos are saved to My videos.

Before you post, check:

- **Identity:** does each face still look like the person in the photo?
- **Placement:** A stays left, B stays right, the mic stays between them.
- **Hands and extras:** no warped fingers, merged faces or extra people.
- **Sound off:** watch once muted — the handoff should still read clearly.

## FAQ

**Do I need CapCut to make the Hotel Lobby AI video?**
No. The video is generated on Hotel Lobby AI with the audio already included. You can still edit it in CapCut afterwards if you want captions.

**Can I make it with a photo of me and a celebrity?**
Only use photos of people who have given you permission. Celebrity likenesses can break platform rules and the person's rights.

**Can I use a pet or a cartoon character?**
The tool is tuned for two real people. Pets and drawings may work but are less reliable.

**How much does it cost?**
Each video uses credits; the exact number is shown on the Generate button and in the [pricing section](/#pricing). Packs are one-time, with no subscription required.
